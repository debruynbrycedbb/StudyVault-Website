# Creating Value Around the Customer: CRM in the Age of AI

**Class presentation summary** · Digital Marketing Tools · MSMSMM · **Lecturer:** Àlex Francés

## Introduction: which brand really knows you?

The session opened by contrasting two kinds of brand experience:

- **“It knows me”:** it recommends what I care about and talks to me at the right moment.
- **“It doesn't know who I am”:** it asks for the same details three times and offers me what I already bought.

The guiding question: what does the first brand have that the second doesn't?

## 01 · What is a CRM today? Defining the customer

### CRM: three letters, one philosophy

- **C – Customer:** the customer
- **R – Relationship:** the relationship
- **M – Management:** managing it

### A CRM is much more than software — four pillars

| Pillar | What it means |
|---|---|
| Strategy | What relationship we want with each customer, and why |
| Data | Complete, clean and connected information |
| Processes | What we do at each customer moment, who and when |
| People | Teams that use it, understand it and feed it |

> Technology + AI is the enabler that centralises, automates and anticipates. Not the goal.

### From contact book to a system that acts

| Era | Stage | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1990s | Contact management | A digital contact book for sales, on in-house servers |
| 2000s | Cloud CRM | SaaS model: subscription and access from anywhere |
| 2010s | Social and omnichannel | Social media, mobile and marketing automation |
| 2020s | Predictive and generative AI | Predicts behaviour and writes content |
| Today | Agentic CRM | AI agents that carry out tasks under human supervision |

### Two key steps before talking about technology

1. Define who our customer is
2. Understand the experience we want to offer
3. Only then: what technology do we need?

### What do we mean by “customer”?

Dictionary definition: a person who buys from a shop or regularly uses the services of a professional or a company. In marketing, “customer” means several roles that are not always held by the same person:

| Role | Who it is |
|---|---|
| Buyer | Who pays |
| User | Who uses or consumes it |
| Decision-maker | Who has the final say |
| Influencer | Who recommends: people… and AI assistants |

### Defining the customer: the case of a soft drink

| Consumer (B2C) | Channel and business (B2B) |
|---|---|
| Out-of-home consumer | HORECA buyers (hospitality) |
| Household shopper | Distributors and retail |
| Young person buying for themselves | Vending companies |
| Family and friends (influence) | HR directors (offices) |
| | Gym managers |

### From static buyer persona to dynamic segment

| Before | Now, with data and AI |
|---|---|
| A buyer persona in a PDF | Segments recalculated with every interaction |
| Reviewed once a year (if you're lucky) | Behaviour + intent + sentiment |
| Demographics: age, city, income | Micro-segments and 1:1 personalisation |
| Fixed, broad segments | AI finds patterns we couldn't see |

## 02 · Customer experience and lifecycle

### What is customer experience (CX)?

*“The sum of all the interactions a customer has with your brand.”*

- **What they do:** search, compare, buy, use, complain
- **What they feel:** frustration, joy, trust, indifference
- **What they remember:** what stays with them… and what they tell others

### Experience as a driver of sales

| Brand | Driver | How |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon | Convenience | One click and frictionless returns |
| Apple | Ritual | The store and the launch as an experience |
| Nike | Community | Apps and membership that personalise the relationship |
| Spotify | Data → content | Wrapped: customers share their own data |

### From reactive to predictive experience

| Approach | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Reactive | We respond when the customer complains | “Open a ticket and wait” |
| Proactive | We get ahead of a known problem | “Your order is late: we've refunded the shipping” |
| Predictive | AI anticipates needs not yet expressed | “You're running out of dog food: shall we send some?” |

- **Sentiment analysis** adjusts the tone and timing of every message.
- **Frustration detection:** AI hands over to a person in time.

### Every touchpoint counts

Customer service · Web · Email · Checkout process · Social media · Reviews · Delivery driver · Advertising · Packaging · App and notifications.

New touchpoints brought by AI: chatbot / AI assistant · what AI says about you. The courier delivering a parcel is often the **“only employee”** the customer meets.

### Experience is built at every stage of the lifecycle

| Stage | Goal | Channels | Teams |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | Attract new customers, identify each lead and prioritise by potential | Social, web, ads, trade fairs, sales team | Marketing · Sales |
| Conversion | Win the first purchase, incentivise it and follow up | Web, store, email, SMS, app, call centre | Sales · Marketing |
| Growth | Repeat purchase, cross-selling and new solutions | Web, store, sales team, app, email | Sales · Service |
| Retention | Prevent churn: spot at-risk customers and act | Customer service, email, app, sales team | Service · Marketing |
| Reactivation | Wake up dormant customers and look after what comes next | Email, SMS, push, direct marketing | Marketing |

### AI at every stage of the lifecycle

| Stage | AI use | What it answers / does |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | Predictive lead scoring | Which leads are most likely to buy? |
| Conversion | Conversational agents | Answer questions at any hour and book demos |
| Growth | Next best offer | What to offer each customer, and when |
| Retention | Churn prediction | An alert before the customer leaves |
| Reactivation | Smart campaigns | The best moment, channel and message for each customer |

### Mini-activity: your worst experience as a customer

In pairs (5 minutes), then 2 or 3 cases shared out loud:

1. What happened, and what stage of the lifecycle were you in?
2. What information did the brand have about you and not use?
3. How would a CRM with AI have solved it… and when was a person needed?

## 03 · Types of CRM and methodology

| Type | What it does |
|---|---|
| Operational | Automates sales, marketing and service processes |
| Analytical | Turns data into knowledge: BI and dashboards |
| Collaborative | A single source of information shared by every team |
| AI-driven / agentic | Predicts, generates content and acts under human supervision |

**By technology:**

- **On-premise:** in-house servers, more control, higher cost
- **Cloud / SaaS:** subscription, continuous updates and built-in AI

### Methodology: every process has four elements

| Element | What it is | With AI |
|---|---|---|
| Target | The customer segment we are addressing | Dynamic segmentation that updates itself |
| Trigger | The action or event that starts the process | Predictive events: act before it happens |
| Content | What we personalise (a promotion? Only if needed) | 1:1 messages generated by AI and reviewed by a person |
| Channel | The medium we communicate through | Picks the best channel and moment for each customer |

### Example: abandoned cart in a fashion e-commerce store

| Element | In this example |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Adds to cart and doesn't buy within 2 hours |
| Target | Registered, opted-in, cart over €50 |
| Content | Products and size in stock. No discount the first time |
| Channel | Email; if unopened after 24 h, an app push |

**What AI adds:** predicts who will buy without an incentive (no margin given away), writes subject and copy for each customer, picks the best send time.

## 04 · What technology do we need?

### What does a CRM do?

| Function | Description |
|---|---|
| Manages | Customer information: details, history, preferences and consents |
| Connects | Marketing, sales and customer service around a single customer view |
| Records | Every interaction: emails, SMS, offers, calls and conversations |
| Automates | Repetitive tasks at every stage of the lifecycle |

### Not everything is a CRM

| System | Focus | Key question |
|---|---|---|
| **CRM** — Customer Relationship Management | The customer relationship, built on internal data | What relationship do we have with this customer? |
| **ERP** — Enterprise Resource Planning | Company resources: stock, invoicing, purchasing | How does the company run inside? |
| **CDP** — Customer Data Platform | Unifies data from several systems into a single profile | Who is this person across all our systems? |
| **MAP** — Marketing Automation Platform | Automates processes and campaigns to convert more | Which message do we send, and when? |

> In 2026 the lines are blurring: what matters is not the acronym but data flowing between systems.

### How to choose a CRM

| Criterion | What to consider |
|---|---|
| What will we use it for? | Specific goals and processes |
| Who will use it? | Team skills and adoption |
| Who will manage it? | IT or the business team itself |
| Budget | Licences + rollout + training. Leave money for actions! |
| GDPR | Consents and where the data is stored |
| Ready for AI? | Built-in AI, data control and clean data |

### CRM must-haves in 2026

| Must-have | Why |
|---|---|
| Automated workflows | Classify contacts, trigger alerts and campaigns |
| Customisation | It adapts to the business, not the other way round |
| Integrations and APIs | Make the most of existing systems |
| Customer service | Tickets and chat in the same view |
| Real-time data | Connected teams with aggregated information |
| Native AI with human control | Assistant, agents, permissions and audit trail |

### The market: every CRM now has its own AI layer

| CRM | AI layer | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Salesforce | Agentforce | Autonomous agents for sales, service and marketing |
| HubSpot | Breeze | Assistant plus customer, prospecting and data agents |
| Microsoft Dynamics 365 | Copilot | Integration with Outlook, Teams and Office |
| Zoho | Zia | Assistant and predictions for SMEs |
| Pipedrive | AI assistant | Suggests next steps in the pipeline |

> 39% of enterprises expect generative AI to reach them through task-automating agents. (Futurum, Enterprise Software survey, Q1 2026)

### Live demo: inside a CRM

1. **Contact record:** the timeline, the memory of the relationship
2. **Deals pipeline:** sales stages, linked to the lifecycle
3. **Lists and segments:** build a segment live (= the Target)

## 05 · AI changes CRM: uses, limits and law

### Three waves of AI in CRM

| Wave | What it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Predictive (machine learning) | Predicts. Who will buy? Who will leave? What is this customer worth? | Lead scoring · Churn · CLV |
| Generative (generative AI) | Creates. Emails, summaries, replies and segments in plain language | Assistants and copilots |
| Agentic (AI agents) | Acts. Carries out tasks end to end under human supervision | Qualify, reply, schedule |

### From rigid chatbot to AI agent

| | Rigid chatbot | AI agent |
|---|---|---|
| Answers… | Predefined questions | Natural language, any question |
| Knowledge | A decision tree | Knowledge base + the customer's CRM data |
| Capability | Informs | Acts: changes a booking, processes a return |
| When it doesn't know… | “Sorry, I didn't understand” | Hands over to a person with full context |
| Main risk | Frustration | Errors and hallucinations → guardrails and supervision |

### Sentiment analysis: how the customer feels

| Customer message | Sentiment | Action |
|---|---|---|
| “This is the third time I've written and nobody answers” | High frustration | Priority + hand over to a person |
| “We loved our stay, we'll be back in summer!” | Positive | Ask for a review · Loyalty offer |
| “Well, the product is fine, nothing special” | Lukewarm | Early churn signal · Follow up |

What it's for: spot churn risk earlier, adjust tone and timing, prioritise and hand over to a person.

### New AI use cases in CRM

| Use case | What it does |
|---|---|
| Automatic insights | The CRM tells you what's happening without asking for a report |
| Customer lifetime value (CLV) | How much to invest in each relationship |
| Next best action | The best thing to do now with each customer |
| 1:1 personalisation | Dynamic segments and individual messages |
| Automatic summaries | Calls and meetings logged to the record automatically |
| Clean data | Spot duplicates and fill empty fields |

### The limits: what AI won't fix

| Limit | Explanation and response |
|---|---|
| Bad data = bad AI | If the CRM is full of duplicates and empty fields, AI amplifies the problem |
| Hallucinations | Confident but false answers → knowledge base and human review |
| Bias | The model reproduces the bias in the data → review the criteria |
| Losing the human touch | Decide which moments will always be human |

### Is it legal? GDPR and the EU AI Act

**GDPR + Spanish LOPDGDD**

- A legal basis for each purpose: freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous consent
- Don't make the contract conditional on unrelated uses
- Data minimisation and transparency
- **Art. 22:** right not to be subject to solely automated decisions

**EU AI Act**

| Date | What applies |
|---|---|
| Feb 2025 | AI literacy and prohibited practices |
| Aug 2026 | Transparency: tell people they are talking to an AI and label generated content |
| Dec 2027 | High-risk systems (e.g. credit scoring), postponed by the Digital Omnibus |

*Not legal advice. Dates as of October 2026.*

### Is it ethical? Three questions before you automate

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| 1. Would the customer expect it? | If they'd be surprised to learn we use that data for this, it's a bad sign |
| 2. Could I explain it openly? | If we can't explain it in one sentence, something is wrong |
| 3. Does it benefit them too? | A good relationship works both ways |

> Trust is a CRM's most valuable asset.

## 06 · Hands-on exercise: CRM + AI Sprint

**The challenge:** you are a brand's CRM team. Design an AI-supported customer relationship programme to solve a specific problem in its lifecycle.

| Format | Details |
|---|---|
| Groups | 4–5 people |
| Time | 23 min of work + 2 min presentation per group |
| Deliverable | The CRM + AI canvas (paper, slide or Miro) |
| Tools | Generative AI as a copilot |
| Schedule | 2:15 Briefing · 2:20 Group work (23 min) · 2:43 Presentations (12 min) · 2:55 Close |

### Choose a brand and its challenge

| Brand | Challenge | Lifecycle stage |
|---|---|---|
| Decathlon | Customers who buy once a year | Growth |
| Sephora | Inactive loyalty programme members | Reactivation |
| Spotify | Users who cancel Premium when the trial ends | Retention |
| Glovo | Customers who order once and never again | Conversion |
| Hotel chain | Guests who only book through online travel agencies | Direct acquisition |
| Business school | Master's prospects who don't enrol | Conversion |

*Hypothetical challenges set for this exercise; not real data from the brands.*

### The CRM + AI canvas

| Box | Questions to answer |
|---|---|
| 1. The customer | 2 key segments. What data do we have and what is missing? |
| 2. Problem and stage | Which lifecycle stage is it in? Which KPI do we want to move? |
| 3. Key moments | 3 critical touchpoints where we lose the customer |
| 4. Automation | Target · Trigger · Content · Channel |
| 5. AI + person | What AI does, and when a person always steps in |
| 6. Risks and law | Data, legal basis, transparency and ethical risk: how do you mitigate them? |

### Use AI as a copilot… with rules

| Step | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Segment | “Act as CRM manager for [brand]. Suggest 3 segments for problem [X] and the data we'd need to identify them” |
| Journey | “Create a synthetic buyer persona for segment [Y] and describe 3 moments where we could lose them” |
| Content | “Write the message for trigger [Z] in two tones, for email and for push. 50 words max” |

**Rules:** no real personal data · what AI says is a hypothesis: validate it · flag which parts AI suggested · AI suggests, the team decides.

## Key takeaways

- AI turns data into relationships.
- CRM moves from managing to anticipating… and acting.
- Value lies in understanding, not storing.
- Trust = consent + transparency + human judgement.

> “Value no longer lies in the data itself, but in what AI allows us to understand and anticipate from it.”
