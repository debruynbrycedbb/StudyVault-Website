## Reading 1 — SEO and AI Search Optimisation

SEO is no longer only about ranking in traditional search engines such as Google and Bing. With the rise of AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, it now includes **AI Search Optimisation**: being visible in standard search results *and* in AI-generated answers and citations.

### Why SEO matters

- **Right moment:** it puts your brand in front of high-intent users at the exact moment of discovery.
- **Higher conversion from AI search:** research shows AI search visitors convert at a significantly higher rate, because they are usually closer to a final decision.
- **Compounding returns:** unlike paid ads or social media, SEO traffic builds over time and gives sustainable, long-term returns.

### The 3 core pillars of SEO

| Pillar | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Technical eligibility (indexation) | Search engines use crawlers to discover content and store it in an index. AI search tools also rely on web content and search indexes to cite sources. | Keep a clean XML sitemap and submit it in Google Search Console (GSC) to check important pages are indexed and fix crawling errors. |
| 2. Keyword and prompt research | Find the terms, questions and phrases your target audience uses, which reveals their search intent. | Check search volume and keyword difficulty (KD %) with tools like the Keyword Magic Tool. Add question-based keywords and AI prompt research to capture conversational queries typed into LLMs. |
| 3. High-quality, intent-driven content | Content must match what the user is actually looking for. | Cover the topic thoroughly, with clear structure, up-to-date data, original insights, mobile-friendly layout and error-free writing. |

### The 4 types of search intent

| Search intent | The user wants to… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Informational | Learn something | "how to make cold brew" |
| Commercial | Compare options | "best running shoes 2026" |
| Navigational | Find a specific site | "Nike login" |
| Transactional | Take an action or buy | "buy specialty coffee beans online" |

## Reading 2 — Long-tail keywords

Long-tail keywords are longer, highly specific phrases that users type when they are close to buying, using voice search, or looking for a precise solution. The name comes from search distribution graphs: a few broad "head" terms get huge volume, but most searches overall are made up of a long "tail" of unique, specific phrases.

### Head terms vs long-tail keywords

| | Head terms | Long-tail keywords |
|---|---|---|
| Example | "furniture", "sofa" | "contemporary art-deco semi-circle lounge" |
| Search volume | Very high per keyword | Low per keyword |
| Visitor quality | Often casual browsers | Highly qualified, close to purchase |
| Conversion rate | Lower | Higher |
| Competition | Fierce | Low: few sites target such specific phrases |
| Cost per click (PPC) | Higher | Significantly lower, with better ROAS |

### The 70% rule

| Keyword type | Share of all web searches |
|---|---|
| Broad head terms | About 10–15% |
| Mid-length keywords | About 15–20% |
| Long-tail variations | Roughly 70% |

### Why they pay off

- **SEO advantage:** because few websites target hyper-specific phrases, smaller brands and startups can rank near the top organically much more easily.
- **PPC advantage:** in paid search (e.g. Google Ads), long-tail keywords face less bidding competition, giving a lower CPC and a better return on ad spend (ROAS).
- **Conversion quality:** less traffic per keyword, but the visitors who arrive are much more likely to buy.

## Reading 3 — Keyword research tools

Keyword research tools help marketers, content creators and SEO professionals find out what audiences search for, judge search volume and competition, and plan which terms to target organically or with paid ads. A strong SEO workflow combines free Google tools as a baseline, a professional all-in-one suite, and specialised niche tools.

### The tool stack

| Category | Tool | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| 1. All-in-one professional suites (enterprise use, deep competitor analysis) | Semrush | One of the most complete platforms. Its Keyword Magic Tool filters huge keyword lists by search intent, difficulty and SERP features. |
| | Ahrefs | Data depth, backlink-informed discovery, and traffic-potential metrics (actual clicks, not just search volume). |
| 2. Foundational and free tools (the baseline) | Google Keyword Planner | The "source of truth" straight from Google Ads: raw search volume, seasonality and CPC estimates. |
| | Google Search Console (GSC) | Auditing your own site: the exact queries, impressions and clicks you already rank for. |
| 3. Affordable and small-business options | SE Ranking | A full, more budget-friendly suite with professional rank tracking and research. |
| | Ubersuggest / KWFinder (Mangools) | Smaller teams, beginners and tight budgets. Simple dashboards built for finding low-competition, long-tail opportunities. |
| 4. Niche and ideation tools | AnswerThePublic / Answer Socrates | Visual maps of question-based searches (how, why, what, versus), useful for natural phrasing, FAQs and conversational prompts. |

### How the three readings connect

1. **Start free:** use Google Search Console to see what you already rank for, and Keyword Planner for volume and CPC.
2. **Go deeper:** use a suite like Semrush or Ahrefs to check difficulty and intent, and to find long-tail gaps competitors miss.
3. **Capture questions:** use AnswerThePublic for question-based and conversational phrasing that also feeds AI prompt research.
4. **Create content** that matches each keyword's intent, so it can rank in search and be cited in AI answers.
