# Lesson 2 — Pre-class reading summaries

**Course:** Digital Marketing and Advertising · MX002

Four readings on the frameworks used to analyse a business and its environment before building a digital marketing plan.

| Framework | Looks at | Main use |
|---|---|---|
| Digital strategy | Internal + external | Prioritise digital investment (7Ds, RACE, BCG rules) |
| SWOT | Internal + external | Summarise strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats |
| Competitive analysis | External (rivals) | Benchmark and sharpen positioning |
| PEST | External (macro) | Scan political, economic, social, tech forces; feeds SWOT O/T |

## Reading 1 — Digital strategy

A digital strategy defines an organisation's priority initiatives for investing in digital technology. Its purpose is to make the business more competitive by digitalising business processes and innovating business models. Beyond technology, it also covers implementation: data governance, resource allocation and change management (often overlapping with a digital transformation strategy).

### 1. Scope: the 7Ds of digital marketing

Digital interactions go far beyond a company website. Strategy creation should harness the 7Ds:

| D | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Digital goals | Clear targets for efficiency and effectiveness |
| Digital audiences | Understanding and managing online customer behaviour |
| Digital devices | Multi-platform access (mobile, desktop, IoT) |
| Digital platforms | Touchpoints such as search engines, social media and apps |
| Digital media | Paid, owned and earned media channels |
| Digital data | Capturing and using customer and operational insights |
| Digital technology | The underlying infrastructure powering the experience |

### 2. Goals of a digital strategy

The overarching goal is to improve business effectiveness by boosting efficiency and operational impact. Though often used interchangeably with "digital marketing strategy", a full digital strategy reaches multiple functions:

- Marketing and e-commerce sales
- New product development and manufacturing
- Supply chain management and procurement
- General operational business management

### 3. How to structure it: the RACE framework

Follows the standard lifecycle (Situation Analysis → Strategy Creation → Implementation), mapped through Smart Insights' RACE Growth System:

- **Opportunity (situation review):** audit current digital maturity; evaluate technology, media and data effectiveness; set baseline objectives and targets.
- **Strategy:** review strategic options and select priority digital initiatives.
- **Action:** build a tactical execution roadmap, often as agile 90-day plans.

### BCG's 5 rules for digital strategy

1. **Assess strategic impact** — understand the changing competitive environment.
2. **Set high ambitions** — energise the organisation for transformation.
3. **Place big bets** — focus on two or three high-value use cases, rolling out short-term wins first.
4. **Build new strategic muscles** — cultivate digital talent and internal skills.
5. **Manage transformation actively** — treat digital change as a structured, ongoing programme.

### 4. Real-world examples

| Company | What they did |
|---|---|
| Adobe | Moved from physical software distribution to the cloud-based Creative Cloud subscription model, changing its business model and operational efficiency (billing, updates). |
| Starbucks | Integrated mobile ordering, payments and loyalty apps to drive rapid mobile sales growth. |
| Renault | Ran 15 digital pilots across functions (marketing to manufacturing) targeting and achieving a 25% increase in EBIT (Earnings Before Interest and Taxes). |

## Reading 2 — SWOT analysis

A SWOT analysis is a four-part strategic framework for evaluating a situation, project, organisation or individual. It splits factors into two **internal** dimensions (what you can control) and two **external** dimensions (what happens in the wider environment).

| | Helpful | Harmful |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | **S — Strengths:** positive attributes and resources within your control. *Organisations:* team capabilities, culture, efficient processes, patents, loyal customer base. *Individuals:* unique skills, professional experience, strong personal habits. | **W — Weaknesses:** factors that detract from your competitive edge or capabilities. *Organisations:* knowledge gaps, outdated tech, poor cash flow, high staff turnover. *Individuals:* skill gaps, poor time management, lack of specific certifications. |
| **External** | **O — Opportunities:** favourable external conditions or trends to exploit for growth. *Examples:* emerging markets, technological shifts, changing consumer preferences, new partnerships, government grants. | **T — Threats:** external elements that could cause trouble or disrupt your plans. *Examples:* aggressive new competitors, economic downturns, shifting regulations, supply chain vulnerabilities. |

### When to use it

- **Business strategy and decision-making:** assessing readiness before entering new markets, rolling out major initiatives or restructuring.
- **Career planning and personal development:** evaluating your career path, preparing for a promotion or navigating role transitions.
- **Problem solving:** getting an objective big-picture view when stuck on a complex problem.

### How to conduct one

1. **Define the objective** — be clear about what you're analysing and why.
2. **Brainstorm strengths** — what you do exceptionally well; be specific and fact-driven.
3. **List weaknesses** — an honest, blame-free appraisal of vulnerabilities and bottlenecks.
4. **Identify opportunities** — scan the external environment for shifts that match your capabilities.
5. **Spot threats** — external obstacles, competitor moves and macroeconomic risks.
6. **Prioritise and take action** — filter to the top 3–5 items per category and turn them into actionable strategies. This is where the real value lies.

### Key advantages

- **Simplicity and flexibility:** easy to understand, quick to apply, scales from individuals to multinationals.
- **Structures complex thoughts:** organises qualitative and quantitative input into a clear visual matrix.
- **Fosters dialogue:** encourages open discussion, realistic self-reflection and cross-functional collaboration.

## Reading 3 — Competitive analysis

A competitive analysis is the structured process of researching and documenting rivals' strategies (products, marketing and sales) to sharpen your own approach.

### The three surfaces to evaluate

| Surface | What to look at |
|---|---|
| What the brand says about itself | Website, marketing messages, sales content |
| What third parties say about it | User reviews, press coverage, industry lists, community forums (e.g. Reddit) |
| What AI search platforms say about it | Whether and how often models like ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity mention or recommend the brand, the sentiment, and which prompts trigger it |

### 1. Types of competitors

- **Direct:** same audience, similar products or services (e.g. DoorDash vs. Uber Eats). This should be your primary focus.
- **Indirect:** different offering, same core customer need (e.g. HelloFresh meal kits vs. food delivery apps).
- **Aspirational:** industry leaders you model your long-term strategy on, even if not yet competing for the same share (e.g. Uber vs. a local delivery startup).

### 2. Why do it

- **Benchmarking performance:** compare web traffic, search rankings, conversion rates and brand presence.
- **Learning from success and failure:** see what works and what backfires to fast-track your own execution.
- **Identifying AI visibility and market gaps:** where rivals win or miss in AI-generated answers, and uncovered segments.
- **Sharpening positioning:** refine your unique value proposition (UVP) against how competitors position themselves.

### 3. How to do it, step by step

1. **Identify and select competitors** — use Google, customer surveys, sales-team insight and SEO tools (e.g. Semrush), then narrow to the three top direct competitors.
2. **Analyse products and services** — compare value propositions, features, capabilities and pricing tier (budget, mid-range, premium) to spot gaps.
3. **Uncover marketing channels and content formats** — where their traffic comes from (organic, referral/partnerships, social, AI) and their content strategy (blogs, videos, newsletters).
4. **Research sales processes** — how they present offers, follow up leads, close and retain customers.
5. **Study customer feedback and ratings** — G2, Trustpilot, Reddit, LinkedIn. Common complaints are direct opportunities to win over unhappy customers.

## Reading 4 — PEST analysis

A PEST analysis (Political, Economic, Socio-cultural, Technological) is a macro-environmental framework. Unlike SWOT, which covers internal and external factors, PEST looks **only at the external macro-environment**: big-picture forces an organisation can't control but must adapt to.

| Factor | What it assesses | Key considerations |
|---|---|---|
| P — Political | How government policy, law and political stability affect operations and regulation | Tax policy, labour laws, environmental regulation, trade restrictions/tariffs, political stability, subsidies and grants |
| E — Economic | The wider economy's effect on purchasing power, costs and demand | Inflation, interest rates, exchange rates, GDP growth, unemployment, disposable income |
| S — Socio-cultural | Demographic shifts, cultural attitudes and trends shaping consumer behaviour | Demographics, age distribution, cultural norms, health consciousness, buying habits, lifestyle changes, attitudes to work and education |
| T — Technological | Innovations that could disrupt the market, create opportunities or make products obsolete | AI and automation, R&D activity, digital infrastructure, cybersecurity, adoption rates, emerging platforms |

### When and why to use it

- **Strategic planning:** spot long-term risks and opportunities before launching a product, entering a market or committing major capital.
- **Risk management:** an early-warning system for external threats, enabling proactive mitigation.
- **Complement to SWOT:** often the main input for the Opportunities and Threats quadrants.

### How to conduct one

1. **Gather data** across all four categories from reliable primary and secondary research (industry reports, economic forecasts, news).
2. **Identify impact and relevance** — which factors have a direct or significant indirect effect on you.
3. **Assess risks and opportunities** — decide whether each factor is an opportunity to exploit or a threat to manage.
4. **Formulate action plans** — build these insights into your core strategic plan and business model.
