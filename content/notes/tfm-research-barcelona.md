## Key findings at a glance

| 343 | 7.9k | 25 h | 8.3m |
|---|---|---|---|
| followers on the boutique account; its own Reels get 431–759 views | median views of the September Barcelona Reels, vs 27.4k for the main account | boutique opening hours a week, vs 40–60 h at nearby Gràcia vintage shops | hotel tourists in Barcelona, Jan–Nov 2025; ≈80% international |

> **The central finding:** the brand is well known in Poland but almost invisible in Barcelona. Barcelona content lost reach from ≈25–28k views per Reel around the opening, to 7.9k for the September collection launch, to under 800 on the boutique's own account. The boutique is open 25 hours a week, has no Google Maps listing, and the website doesn't mention its address, hours or euro prices.

- **Market:** 8.3m hotel tourists (Jan–Nov 2025; top markets US, UK, France, Italy, Germany); shopping ≈18% of tourists' daily spend. Gràcia has 126,000 young residents (average age 34), a quarter foreign.
- **Consumers:** 3 in 5 Spaniards have bought second-hand clothes, but price is the main reason (64%). Instagram is where Spaniards research products most (45%).
- **Competition:** vintage shops (Beyondwear, Revolution Vintage, Vintage Gracia) and a handmade upcycling studio (CRYPTA) within walking distance, most open 40–60 hours a week. Free public upcycling workshops also exist.
- **Positioning risk:** October posts use #vintagebarcelona and #thriftbarcelona, putting the boutique in the cheapest, most crowded category.

**Recommended focus:** 1) make the boutique findable · 2) position it as an upcycling atelier and experience · 3) build a Barcelona audience on Instagram · 4) plan around Primavera Sound, Festes de Gràcia and 080 Barcelona Fashion.

**What it means:** English first (US/UK visitors), then Spanish and Catalan for locals. Tourist growth has flattened and taxes are rising, so the boutique shouldn't depend on tourists alone. A ≈€176 jacket can't compete on price with a vintage rail — sell the design, the making and the experience.

Wider competition: old-town vintage cluster (Humana, L'Arca, Le Swing, Moda re-, Flamingos), sustainable boutiques (Sunsais, The Good Store, Green Life Style), Barcelona upcycling designers (Tania Marcial, CRYPTA), free public workshops ("Transforma la teva roba!").

> **Insight:** what nobody nearby combines is an internationally shown designer, a story-led space and hands-on customisation. The boutique wins by selling that, not by competing as one more vintage shop.

## Instagram for Barcelona

| Phase | Reels | Median views | Caption language |
|---|---|---|---|
| Announcement (Feb–Apr) | 4 | 27,800 | Mostly English |
| Opening series (May–Jun) | 5 | 24,800 | English |
| ReMade launch (Sep) | 3 | 7,869 | Polish |
| Boutique account (Oct) | 3 | 649 | English |

- **Started strong, then faded.** By September the Barcelona collection Reels fell to a median of 7.9k.
- **The audience is in the wrong country.** September Reels were captioned in Polish; people who saw them engaged well (≈3.6% per view), so the problem is distribution, not content.
- **The boutique account can't carry the shop yet.** Its own Reels reach 431–759 views; June collab posts reached 19–29k only because the main account co-posted them.
- **Proven formats haven't been used in Barcelona:** dressing well-known people (≈300k median), festivals (≈270k), humour (≈61k).
- **Demand is lost in DMs:** price and size questions go to private messages; almost no Story Highlights.

**Recommended primary focus:** opportunities 1–3 together — cheap, immediate, and they create the measurable funnel (discovery → visit → purchase or booking) the Consumer Decision Journey in Block 2 needs.
